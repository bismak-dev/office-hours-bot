# Lecture 3 — Correlation and Causation (SAMPLE SLIDES)

## Slide 2: Correlation
Correlation measures how strongly two variables move together, from -1 to 1. A correlation near 0 means little linear relationship.

## Slide 3: Correlation is not causation
Two variables can be correlated because a third variable, called a confounder, affects both. For example, ice cream sales and sunburns rise together because of hot weather, not because one causes the other.

## Slide 4: How to establish causation
Randomized experiments such as A/B tests are the most reliable way to show that one thing causes another.
